# Loyalty, Corruption & Exposure

The three faction trackers turn relationships, leverage, and evidence into playable pressure. They are public records for the player whose agent is affected. They are not alignment scores, secret punishment meters, or substitutes for what happens in the fiction.

- **Loyalty** runs from 0 to 10 and records the Division's observable trust, access, and willingness to protect the agent.
- **Corruption** runs from 0 to 10 and records the total strength of active claims held by Zhou's network.
- **Exposure** runs from 0 to 6 and records active evidence connecting the agent to unauthorized activity.

The tracks are independent. An agent may have high Loyalty and high Corruption, low Loyalty and no Corruption, or significant Exposure without the Division yet believing the allegation. A single choice can affect more than one track, but each change needs its own cause.

## Open Information and Ownership
<!-- UTRULE: TRACK-05 | PRINCIPLE | Tracker causality -->

Each agent owns their three scores and the ledger entries supporting them. The Warden may keep the identity of an informant, the location of a recording, or another discoverable detail secret when the fiction requires it, but must tell the player:

- which tracker changed;
- by how much;
- the kind of fact supporting the change;
- which faction or institution can act on it;
- what the agent could do to learn more or respond.

Do not change a tracker in secret. Hidden information can create uncertainty; hidden rules cannot create informed choices.

Trackers are personal by default. When evidence or a faction claim implicates several agents, record the same shared entry on the Team Campaign Sheet and list every implicated agent. Each agent's score changes only by the amount that applies to them.

## The Faction Ledger

A number without a supporting fact cannot generate a fair scene. Whenever a tracker changes, create or update a ledger entry.

| Tracker | Required ledger entry |
|---|---|
| Loyalty | Observable act, who can verify it, resulting access or restriction |
| Corruption | Claim holder, proof or dependency, strength, possible demand, release condition |
| Exposure | Evidence source, holder, weight, link to the agent, possible countermeasure |

Record changes when the fact becomes true. Resolve threshold scenes at the first plausible moment, normally during debriefing, settlement, downtime, or the opening of the next mission rather than interrupting an unrelated action.

All three scores have hard limits. Loyalty and Corruption cannot fall below 0 or rise above 10. Exposure cannot fall below 0 or rise above 6. Facts beyond a cap still matter and should be recorded as aggravating circumstances, additional claims, or unresolved evidence.

## Loyalty: Institutional Trust and Access
<!-- UTRULE: TRACK-01 | RULE | Loyalty -->

Loyalty measures the Division's current institutional position toward the agent. It does not measure affection, patriotism, obedience in the abstract, or moral worth. A senior official may personally dislike a highly trusted agent; the institution can still rely on that agent's record, access, and usefulness.

### Changing Loyalty

Change Loyalty only when the Division observes an important act or receives credible evidence of it. Routine mission completion earns its listed payment and does not automatically raise Loyalty.

| Change | Use when |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A significant act changes a report, supervisor's judgment, or access decision |
| 2 | A major success, breach, sacrifice, or deception changes the agent's institutional status |
| 3 | An exceptional campaign event saves, compromises, or openly betrays the Division |

Typical gains include completing an unusually difficult authorized objective, protecting civilians at real cost, surrendering a major unregistered asset, revealing an inconvenient truth during debriefing, or defending another agent through accountable official channels.

Typical losses include a proven false report, preventable abandonment, misuse of restricted resources, refusal of a lawful assignment without explanation, an unauthorized handover the Division can prove, or a failure concealed through fabricated evidence.

A failed mission is not automatically disloyal. A successful mission is not automatically loyal. The question is what the Division can verify about the agent's conduct.

### Loyalty Bands

| Loyalty | Institutional position and default permissions |
|---:|---|
| 0-1 | **Breach.** No routine sensitive access. Detention, suspension, desertion, prosecution, or active pursuit must be resolved as an explicit scene. Essential medicine is not silently removed; any denial or interruption is a stated consequence. |
| 2-3 | **Restricted.** Assignments narrow, reports receive review, sensitive equipment requires authorization, and supervision is common. |
| 4-6 | **Cleared.** Standard assignments, issued equipment, ordinary medical access, and routine professional protection. |
| 7-8 | **Trusted.** The agent can normally obtain a plausible restricted briefing, specialist consultation, priority appointment, or official cover when the Division has the capacity to provide it. |
| 9-10 | **Essential asset.** Senior officials will normally hear the agent, defend their continued field status, and grant strategic access within the agent's role. The Division also entrusts them with greater responsibility and harder consequences. |

Loyalty is not spent like currency. When an agent requests access, use the current band as a fictional permission:

1. State the requested resource, information, protection, or intervention and why the Division could provide it.
2. If the request is ordinary for the band and no established official opposes it, grant it without a save.
3. If a specific official has reason to resist and the outcome is uncertain and consequential, resolve that pressure with a WIL save or direct negotiation.
4. A refusal does not reduce Loyalty by itself. A later act, report, or confrontation may change the score for an observable reason.

High Loyalty can change who handles an accusation or which options remain available. It does not erase evidence, cancel Exposure, or make the agent immune to consequences.

## Corruption: Zhou's Active Claims
<!-- UTRULE: TRACK-02 | RULE | Corruption claims -->

Corruption is the combined strength of the enforceable claims Zhou's network holds over an agent. It is not supernatural evil, personal vice, or a judgment about why the agent accepted help.

A transaction changes Corruption only when it creates or strengthens a continuing claim. Clean payment for a completed exchange may create no Corruption. Credit, concealment, protection, incriminating proof, dependency, a threatened relationship, or an enforceable promise usually does.

### The Claim Record

Every active claim records five elements:

1. **Holder:** Zhou or the named broker, clinic, creditor, fixer, or lieutenant who can act.
2. **Basis:** the favor, evidence, dependency, protection, secret, or promise supporting the claim.
3. **Strength:** 1, 2, or 3.
4. **Demand:** one or more requests the claim could credibly support.
5. **Release condition:** repayment, evidence destruction, extraction of a threatened person, exposure of the holder, or another action that genuinely removes the leverage.

Corruption equals the total strength of all active claims, to a maximum of 10.

| Claim strength | Scope |
|---:|---|
| 1 | **Limited.** A small favor, traceable transaction, short-term credit, minor secret, or single-use access obligation. |
| 2 | **Serious.** Incriminating proof, essential treatment, substantial credit, a protected dependent, repeated concealment, or a betrayal that can damage the agent's career. |
| 3 | **Dominant.** A campaign-changing dependency, life-saving intervention, evidence of treason, control over someone central to the agent, or an obligation that can reshape several missions. |

Do not add a new claim for every conversation about the same fact. Increase an existing claim only when Zhou acquires stronger proof, wider control, or a new dependency. Separate facts may create separate claims.

### Corruption Bands

| Corruption | Zhou's practical position |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No active claim. Zhou may make offers but cannot demand repayment. |
| 1-2 | One or more limited claims. Zhou can request a proportionate favor and remind the agent what remains unresolved. |
| 3-4 | Meaningful leverage. Refusal can activate a named consequence attached to a claim. |
| 5-6 | Deep leverage. Claims can affect missions, medicine, employment, evidence, family, or a protected community. |
| 7-8 | Severe control. Zhou can threaten major personal or institutional harm, though escape remains possible through a plan and sacrifice. |
| 9-10 | Ownership in practice. Several routes out may exist, but none is clean, private, or cheap. |

A claim does not control the agent's decision. Zhou states a demand and the consequence she can credibly activate. The player decides whether the agent complies, refuses, bargains, discloses the claim, attacks its basis, or accepts the consequence.

Fulfilling a demand does not automatically remove the claim. Clear or reduce it only when the agreed release condition is met or the leverage itself is neutralized. If a strength-2 claim loses part of its basis but remains useful, reduce it to strength 1 and rewrite the demand.

## Exposure: the Evidence Clock
<!-- UTRULE: TRACK-03 | RULE | Exposure evidence -->

Exposure is a visible 0-6 clock built from active evidence. It measures how close unauthorized activity is to producing institutional action. It does not measure guilt, public reputation, or how suspicious the Warden personally finds the agent.

### The Evidence Record

Every Exposure increase must name an evidence source, holder, weight, link to the agent, and possible countermeasure. Exposure equals the total weight of active evidence, to a maximum of 6.

| Weight | Evidence quality |
|---:|---|
| 1 | **Linked.** One witness, one traceable payment, one suspicious record, one missing item, or a pattern that supports scrutiny but is contestable. |
| 2 | **Corroborated.** Independent sources agree, an official system contains the trace, the event was public, or the evidence directly connects the agent to serious unauthorized activity. |

Do not count the same trace twice. A second copy of the same recording is not new evidence; an independent witness confirming it may increase the entry from weight 1 to 2. A clean act with no surviving record creates no Exposure even if it was unauthorized. A catastrophic public breach normally creates one weight-2 entry and may create additional independent entries.

### Scrutiny at Exposure 3

When Exposure first reaches or crosses 3, introduce one **scrutiny response** at the first plausible opportunity. Tie it to a named evidence entry.

Possible responses include:

- a report or payroll audit;
- an assigned observer;
- an interview with stated allegations;
- temporary restriction of one relevant access privilege;
- pressure on a witness or contact;
- a controlled assignment intended to test the agent's account.

Scrutiny is a scene or ongoing condition, not an automatic numerical penalty. Crossing 3 does not itself reduce Loyalty. Loyalty changes only if the Division learns something that alters trust.

If Exposure falls below 3 and later crosses it again because new evidence appears, introduce a new scrutiny response. Remaining at 3-5 does not generate a fresh response every week unless an existing actor advances the case for a known reason.

### Investigation at Exposure 6

When Exposure reaches 6, mark the investigation as pending. Record further evidence as aggravating facts, but do not raise the number above 6. Run the investigation at the first plausible opportunity.

Use this procedure:

1. **Name the actor.** Identify the investigator, superior, rival faction, creditor, prosecutor, or blackmailer acting on the evidence.
2. **State the case.** Tell the player what is alleged, what is already known, and which evidence supports it.
3. **State the immediate stakes.** Search, suspension, detention, loss of access, exposure of a contact, prosecution, forced cooperation, or another concrete outcome.
4. **Ask for a response.** The agent may cooperate, disclose, contest, produce authorization, redirect blame, bargain, flee, or pursue another plausible method.
5. **Roll only for uncertainty.** Do not roll to erase established evidence. Use saves when a risky response has an uncertain and consequential result.
6. **Resolve every affected tracker separately.** Exposure changes with the remaining evidence; Loyalty changes only with the Division's resulting judgment; Corruption changes only if Zhou gains or loses a claim.
7. **Set Exposure from what remains.** Use 0 for complete exoneration or destruction of the case, 1-2 for contained residual evidence, and 3-5 for an active unresolved case. A partially contained investigation normally returns to 2.

The investigation must change the situation. It may clear the agent, impose oversight, expose another faction, split the team, create a legal problem, force a bargain, or turn the agent into a fugitive. It should never be reduced to a single punishment roll.

### Reducing Exposure

Exposure falls only when evidence is genuinely neutralized. The agents may:

- prove that the act was authorized or that the evidence identifies someone else;
- destroy or recover a record before another holder copies it;
- persuade a witness to recant for a credible reason;
- repair a contradictory report with verifiable facts;
- expose a corrupt investigator and invalidate the case;
- obtain an institutional order that lawfully seals or closes the record;
- accept Zhou's cleanup, creating or strengthening a Corruption claim if her network gains leverage.

One downtime action can pursue one concrete evidence objective. If the method is safe and sufficient, resolve it without a save. If it is possible, uncertain, dangerous, and consequential, use the relevant save. Spending an action does not automatically reduce Exposure.

## How the Trackers Interact

Keep the cause of each track separate.

- **Loyalty does not cancel Exposure.** High Loyalty may secure counsel, time, access, or a sympathetic investigator, but the evidence remains until addressed.
- **Exposure does not automatically reduce Loyalty.** It changes Loyalty only when the Division receives and believes information that alters trust.
- **Corruption does not automatically raise Exposure.** A private claim can remain invisible. It raises Exposure only when evidence of the underlying activity exists.
- **Zhou can reduce Exposure only by changing the evidence.** Her assistance normally creates or strengthens a claim when it gives her proof, dependency, or a right to repayment.
- **The same act may change several tracks.** Surrendering an unregistered relic could raise Loyalty because the Division verifies the disclosure, reduce Exposure because the custody discrepancy is resolved, and leave Corruption unchanged because Zhou gains nothing.

Do not trade tracker points directly. An agent cannot spend Loyalty to remove Exposure or pay Cash to erase Corruption unless the fiction establishes the access, evidence, claim, and result of that action.

## Double Exposure
<!-- UTRULE: TRACK-04 | RULE | Double Exposure -->

**Double Exposure applies while Loyalty is 6 or higher and Corruption is 4 or higher.** The agent has enough official access to be useful and enough Zhou leverage to be vulnerable.

Double Exposure is not another score and does not force betrayal. When it begins, prepare one pressure packet:

1. **Official access:** identify information, equipment, authority, or proximity the agent possesses because of Loyalty.
2. **Active claim:** choose a Zhou claim that can credibly reach that access.
3. **Incompatible requests:** state what the Division wants and what Zhou wants. Both must be possible, concrete, and meaningfully incompatible.
4. **Evidence risk:** identify what would raise Exposure if the agent attempts concealment, diversion, or simultaneous compliance.
5. **Deadline:** place the choice inside a mission clock, settlement, meeting, or coming event.

The player may disclose the claim, refuse either side, negotiate a third arrangement, cooperate with an investigation, satisfy one request, or attempt to satisfy both. Apply only the consequences the factions can actually produce.

Double Exposure ends when Loyalty falls below 6 or Corruption falls below 4. Ending the condition does not erase existing claims, evidence, promises, or consequences.

## Offers, Requests, and Demands

Use the faction's actual position rather than treating every approach the same.

| Approach | Requirements | Meaning of refusal |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | No claim or authority required | The offer ends or changes; no automatic tracker loss |
| Request | Relationship, access, or a limited claim supports the ask | The relationship may cool or the claim may remain unresolved, but consequences must be named |
| Demand | A serious claim, institutional order, or immediate power supports compliance | Refusal activates the stated consequence if the faction can carry it out |

A lawful order may still be refused. A powerful claim may still be defied. These trackers describe pressure and consequence, not control of a player character.

## Worked Example: The Altered Route Log

Mara begins the week at Loyalty 7, Corruption 3, and Exposure 2. Her ledger shows a strength-2 Zhou claim based on unregistered clinic treatment and a strength-1 claim based on a favor owed to a courier. Exposure comes from a weight-2 surveillance entry linking her to the clinic.

Zhou asks Mara to replace a Division convoy route in the logistics system. In exchange, Zhou will erase the clinic footage and close the strength-2 claim. Mara agrees only after Zhou's courier provides the deletion key.

The choice does not change the trackers all at once.

1. Zhou provides the key and permanently destroys the clinic footage. The strength-2 claim loses its basis, so Corruption falls from 3 to 1. The weight-2 clinic evidence also disappears, so Exposure falls from 2 to 0.
2. Mara alters the route log. Zhou now possesses the authenticated replacement request and can prove Mara made the change. This creates a new strength-2 claim, raising Corruption from 1 to 3.
3. The altered log remains inside the Division system. It creates one weight-1 evidence entry, raising Exposure from 0 to 1.
4. Mara still has Loyalty 7 because the Division has not discovered the alteration. Her official trust did not change merely because the player knows what she did.

If an auditor later corroborates the log with camera footage, that evidence becomes weight 2 and Exposure rises to 2. If a second independent witness connects Mara to the convoy diversion, Exposure rises to 3 and scrutiny begins. Only then might the resulting report also change Loyalty.

This sequence keeps every number attached to who knows what, what they can prove, and what they can do next.

## Session and Settlement Checklist

At debriefing, settlement, and session close, ask:

1. What observable act changed the Division's trust or access?
2. Which Zhou claims were created, strengthened, weakened, fulfilled, or destroyed?
3. Which evidence entries appeared, changed weight, moved to another holder, or were neutralized?
4. Did Loyalty cross into a new institutional band?
5. Did Exposure cross 3 or reach 6?
6. Does Double Exposure now begin or end?
7. Which faction scene follows from the ledger, and when will it occur?

The score indicates the scale of pressure. The ledger supplies the next playable scene.
