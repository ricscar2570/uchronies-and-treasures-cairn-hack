## Core Rules

### Saves

A **save** is a roll to avoid bad outcomes from risky choices and circumstances. Roll a **d20**. If you roll **equal to or under** the relevant attribute, you succeed. Otherwise, you fail. A **1** is always a success. A **20** is always a failure.

When a save is made with **advantage**, roll 2d20 and keep the **lower** result (better for the player). When made with **disadvantage**, roll 2d20 and keep the **higher** result (worse for the player).

Outside attacks, a named **impaired** action means disadvantage on its risky save; an **enhanced** action means advantage. Neither requires a roll when the fiction already resolves the action or withholds freely available information. Multiple sources of advantage do not add dice; the same is true of disadvantage. If both apply, roll normally.

**STR saves:** Resisting poison, enduring pain, forcing a door, absorbing a physical impact.

**DEX saves:** Dodging an explosion, acting before an enemy, sneaking past guards, keeping your balance.

**WIL saves:** Resisting temporal contamination, seeing through a lie, maintaining composure under interrogation, persuading someone who doesn't trust you.

> When no save is needed, the Warden simply decides based on the fiction. If your background as an ex-military means you'd know how to field-strip a weapon, you just do it. No roll.

### Healing

**HP** recovers after a few moments of rest and a drink of water. This leaves the party exposed (the Warden may trigger an encounter).

**Attribute damage** (STR, DEX, or WIL loss) requires **a week of rest** facilitated by a healer or medical care. Division medical services are available but slow (1 week). At Loyalty 7+, priority care restores attributes in 3 days. Black market healing is faster but costs $200 to $500. **Contamination damage to WIL is a specific exception:** a completed week of Division care restores d6 WIL, or d8 after 3 days at Loyalty 7+; paid temporal therapy restores d6 after 2 days. It does not restore all WIL automatically.

A character who lacks a crucial need (food, water, rest, anti-rejection medicine) is **Deprived**. A Deprived character cannot recover HP or attributes.

### Fatigue

Anyone Deprived for more than a day adds **Fatigue** to their inventory, one per day. Each Fatigue occupies one inventory slot and lasts until the character recovers in safety (a full night's rest in a safe place).

Temporal contamination and certain Quirk effects can also cause Fatigue.

### Armor

Before calculating damage to HP, subtract the target's **Armor** value from the damage roll. No one can have more than **3 Armor** total, regardless of how many pieces of protection they wear.

|---|---|---|---|
| Light tactical vest (kevlar II) | 1 | Division issue | Standard issue |
| Ballistic vest | 2 | $1,500 | Black market, bulky |
| Heavy tactical armor | 3 | $3,000 | Zhou only, bulky |
| Helmet | +1 | $200 | Stacks with vest (max 3 total) |

### Reactions

When the PCs encounter an NPC whose reaction is not obvious, the Warden rolls **2d6**:

| 2d6 | Reaction |
|---|---|
| 2 | Hostile |
| 3-5 | Wary |
| 6-8 | Cautious |
| 9-11 | Cooperative |
| 12 | Helpful |

### Morale

Enemies must make a **WIL save** to avoid fleeing when they take their **first casualty** and again when they lose **half their number**. Groups may use their leader's WIL. Lone foes save when reduced to 0 HP. Morale does not apply to PCs.

### Rounds and Initiative

When timing matters, use **rounds** (roughly 10 seconds). On their turn, a character may move up to **40 feet** and take **one action**: attack, use an item, move again, or any other reasonable action.

At the start of combat, each PC makes a **DEX save** to act before their opponents. PCs who succeed take an opening turn before the enemies. Then the enemies act, followed by all PCs; continue alternating enemies and all PCs. A failed opening save delays the PC until after the first enemy turn; it does not remove their turn after that enemy action. Special circumstances (ambush, superior position) may let one side act automatically first.

### Attacking and Damage

**Attacks automatically hit.** The attacker rolls their weapon's damage die and subtracts the target's Armor, to a minimum of 0 damage. The remainder is applied to the target's HP. Unarmed attacks always deal **d4** damage.

> *Example: Reyes fires her Glock 17 (d6) at a scavenger wearing a leather jacket (1 Armor). She rolls a 5. Minus 1 Armor = 4 damage to the scavenger's HP.*

- **Multiple attackers (same target):** Roll all damage dice and keep the **single highest result**.
- **Dual weapons:** Roll both damage dice and keep the **single highest result**.
- **Impaired:** Fighting from a position of weakness (shooting through the target's cover, restrained, blinded). Roll **d4** damage regardless of weapon.
- **Enhanced:** Fighting from a position of advantage (point-blank against a helpless target, ambush). Roll **d12** damage regardless of weapon.
- **Blast:** Affects all targets in the noted area. Roll damage separately for each. Explosives, grenades, and certain temporal anomalies have blast.

### Critical Damage

Damage that reduces a target's HP **below zero** applies the remainder to **STR**. The target must then make a **STR save using their reduced current STR** to avoid **critical damage**. On a failure, they collapse and can only crawl weakly. If given aid within an hour, they stabilize. If left untreated, they die.

> *Example: Chen has 2 HP. A guard shoots him with a rifle (d8) and rolls 6. After subtracting 1 Armor, that's 5 damage. 2 HP are absorbed, 3 go to STR. Chen had 11 STR and now has 8. He must save against 8, not 11. He rolls 14. Failure. Chen collapses, bleeding. His teammates have one hour.*

### Attribute Loss

- **STR reaches 0:** *Death*.
- **DEX reaches 0:** *Paralysis*.
- **WIL reaches 0 from contamination:** *Temporal echo*. The character is lost forever.
- **WIL reaches 0 otherwise:** *Catatonia*. Requires a week of medical care.

### Detachments

Large groups fight as a single **detachment**. Critical damage routes them; 0 STR destroys them. Attacks against detachments by individuals are **impaired** (d4, unless blast). Attacks by detachments against individuals are **enhanced** (d12) and deal **blast** damage.

### Retreat

Running from danger requires a successful **DEX save** and a safe destination to run to.

### Death and Replacement

When a character dies, the player creates a new character or takes control of a hireling. The new character joins the party immediately. The Warden and player decide how: a Division replacement, a scavenger who joins, or a prisoner freed during the mission. The new PC starts at Recruit tier with Loyalty 5, Corruption 0, Certificates 0, no Quirks, $500 Cash ($1,000 for a Con Artist), $500 personal Debt and normal starting equipment. They do not inherit the dead PC's personal Debt. Explicit team obligations remain with the team; joining does not silently assign a financial share.

**Ammunition.** Track reloads, not individual bullets. Each firearm has one current magazine (**ready**, **low**, or **empty**) and a number of compatible spare reloads. A new pistol starts ready, with **2 spare reloads**. The loaded magazine uses no slot beyond the weapon; every two spares occupy 1 slot, rounding up.

After an intense firefight, roll **one d6 for each firearm that was fired**. On **1**, its current magazine becomes empty. On **2**, ready becomes low; a second 2 while low makes it empty. On **3-6**, nothing changes; a low magazine remains low. This check does not retroactively cancel shots already resolved. A brief exchange does not require a check unless the Warden announced sustained fire before it began.

An empty firearm cannot fire. Spend one spare to load it and return it to ready; loading takes one action in danger, or a moment in safety. Having no spares does not prevent firing a ready or low current magazine. A spare costs **$50 for one compatible reload**, not a resupply of the whole arsenal. Two firearms track separate magazines and pools unless their ammunition is explicitly compatible.

### Scars

When a single attack reduces a PC from positive HP to **exactly 0 HP**, use the **damage actually taken from that attack, after Armor**, as the Scars table result. **Do not roll d12.** Use result 12 for damage above 12. Resolve the indicated Scar once; any dice named in that entry are still rolled.

Example: 4 HP, Armor 1, incoming roll 5: take 4 damage, reach exactly 0 HP, use Scar 4. Incoming roll 6 instead deals 5: 4 to HP and 1 to STR, followed by a save against reduced STR, with no Scar from that hit. An attack against an already-0-HP PC, or filling all inventory slots, does not trigger Scars.

| Damage taken | Scar |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Lasting Scar.** Roll d6 for location (1 Neck, 2 Hands, 3 Eye, 4 Chest, 5 Legs, 6 Ear). Roll d6; if higher than max HP, take the new result. |
| 2 | **Rattling Blow.** Disoriented and shaken. Roll d6; if higher than max HP, take the new result. |
| 3 | **Winded.** Sent flying. Deprived until you rest a few hours, then roll d6 and add to max HP. |
| 4 | **Broken Limb.** Roll d6 (1-2 Leg, 3-4 Arm, 5 Rib, 6 Skull). Once mended, roll 2d6; if higher than max HP, take the new result. |
| 5 | **Infected.** Gross infection. When you recover, roll 2d6; if higher than max HP, take the new result. |
| 6 | **Head Wound.** Roll d6 (1-2 STR, 3-4 DEX, 5-6 WIL). Roll 3d6; if higher than that ability, take the new result. |
| 7 | **Hamstrung.** Can barely move without help. After recovery, roll 3d6; if higher than max DEX, take the new result. |
| 8 | **Deafened.** Cannot hear until extraordinary aid. Make a WIL save; on a pass, increase max WIL by d4. |
| 9 | **Concussed.** Part of your psyche is knocked loose. Roll 3d6; if higher than max WIL, take the new result. |
| 10 | **Dismembered.** An appendage is torn off or crippled (Warden decides which). Make a WIL save; on a pass, increase max WIL by d6. |
| 11 | **Mortal Wound.** Deprived and out of action. Die in one hour unless healed. On recovery, roll 2d6 as new max HP. |
| 12 | **Doomed.** You survived, barely. If your next critical damage save fails, you die horribly. On a pass, roll 3d6; if higher than max HP, take the new result. |
