# Core Rules

These rules answer three questions: **does an uncertain action expose someone to a consequence, what happens when violence begins, and what remains after the danger passes?** The Warden starts from the fiction, not from a target number. Dice resolve risk; they do not replace information, preparation, or judgment.

> **Core sequence:** describe the situation, establish what is possible, state the danger, make one relevant roll if needed, and change the situation according to the result.

## Attributes and Saves

The three attributes describe different forms of resistance and control.

| Attribute | Use it when the immediate risk tests... | Common examples |
|---|---|---|
| **STR** | force, endurance, bodily integrity | forcing a door under pressure, resisting poison, enduring pain, holding a collapsing barrier |
| **DEX** | speed, balance, precision, concealment | crossing unstable ground, acting before an enemy, avoiding an explosion, moving silently past a guard |
| **WIL** | resolve, perception, social control, temporal integrity | resisting contamination, maintaining a deception, pressuring an uncertain witness, keeping composure under interrogation |

A **save** is a roll to avoid a harmful outcome created by a risky choice or dangerous circumstance. Make a save only when the action is:

- possible;
- uncertain;
- dangerous;
- consequential.

If any one of those conditions is absent, do not roll. A safe professional task succeeds. An impossible action remains impossible until the situation changes. A question about information the agent could simply perceive receives an honest answer.

### Making a Save
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-01 | RULE | Saving throws -->

Roll **d20** and compare the result with the relevant **current** attribute.

- Equal to or under the attribute: success.
- Over the attribute: failure.
- A natural **1** always succeeds.
- A natural **20** always fails.

Before the roll, the Warden makes the immediate danger and its apparent severity legible. The exact result follows from the method, position, and hazard: lost time, injury, detection, a damaged tool, separation, a worsened bargain, or simple failure to achieve the objective.

A failed save resolves the attempt. Do not repeat the same roll until the agents change their method, tools, position, or circumstances.

> **Example:** Mara can open a standard evidence locker with the correct key and enough time. No save is needed. If the key is bent and security is approaching, she makes a DEX save. Success opens the locker before security arrives. Failure leaves it locked; the attempt consumes the available time and security seals the corridor.

### Choosing Who Saves

The person most exposed to the consequence makes the save.

- If one agent acts against an NPC, the side most at risk saves. Do not make opposed rolls.
- If two agents must perform one action together, the one most endangered by failure saves, usually the agent with the lower relevant attribute.
- If every agent independently faces the same hazard, each affected agent saves.
- If one agent's success would protect the whole team, make one save and apply the established hazard to everyone exposed.

A save never allows one character to control another player character's decision. Player-versus-player coercion is resolved by agreement at the table, not by WIL rolls.

### Advantage and Disadvantage
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-02 | RULE | Advantage and disadvantage -->

When circumstances strongly favor the agent, roll with **advantage**: roll 2d20 and keep the lower result.

When circumstances strongly hinder the agent, roll with **disadvantage**: roll 2d20 and keep the higher result.

Advantage and disadvantage are states, not accumulating bonuses. Multiple favorable circumstances still produce only advantage. Multiple unfavorable circumstances still produce only disadvantage. If both apply, they cancel and the agent rolls one d20.

Use them sparingly. Better preparation often changes what is possible, removes the save, reduces exposure to danger, or creates an entirely different approach.

### Assistance
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-03 | RULE | Assistance -->

Another agent can help when they can describe a concrete contribution: holding the door, monitoring the sensor, distracting the witness, or guiding the injured climber. Effective help may grant advantage, remove an obstacle, or reduce the consequence. The helper shares any consequence that naturally follows from being involved.

Several people saying "I help" does not create additional dice. Establish what each person actually does and whether the fiction changes.

## The Fate Die
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-04 | RULE | Fate Die -->

When the table needs an answer about chance rather than a character's risky action, roll a **Fate Die (d6)**.

- **4-6:** the result generally favors the agents.
- **1-3:** the result generally creates trouble or favors an opposing interest.

Use the Fate Die for questions such as whether a forgotten service door is unlocked, which faction arrives first when either is plausible, or whether an old vehicle starts before the storm reaches it. Do not use it to hide information already established by preparation or to replace a save when an agent is directly at risk.

## HP, Attributes, and Harm
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-05 | RULE | HP and attribute harm -->

**HP means Hit Protection**, not physical health. It represents awareness, movement, luck, and the ability to turn a dangerous hit into a near miss or glancing impact. Losing HP can look like shredded cover, a bruising fall, a narrow dodge, or mounting exhaustion.

Attribute loss is more serious.

- **STR loss** usually represents wounds, poison, or bodily trauma.
- **DEX loss** represents impaired movement, nerve damage, or physical disorientation.
- **WIL loss** represents mental collapse, temporal contamination, or damage to the agent's ability to remain themselves.

Record current and maximum values separately. Saves use the current value. No current value can exceed its maximum unless a rule explicitly raises that maximum.

### Direct Attribute Damage

Some hazards bypass HP because awareness and combat positioning cannot meaningfully soften them. Poison may damage STR, a neurological weapon may damage DEX, and contamination damages WIL. The rule or the Warden states the affected attribute and damage before the agent commits whenever the danger could be recognized.

Armor reduces damage only when the protection is relevant. A ballistic vest does not protect WIL from a temporal fracture or STR from inhaled poison.

## Healing and Recovery
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-06 | RULE | Healing and recovery -->

A few quiet minutes, relative safety, and water restore HP to its maximum. This cannot be done during a combat round or while an immediate threat is actively preventing rest. When site time matters, recovery requires one uninterrupted exploration turn and advances all clocks normally, including scheduled contamination time.

Ordinary attribute recovery requires appropriate treatment and a full week of rest. It normally consumes both downtime actions and restores the treated attribute to its maximum unless a specific injury says otherwise. Faster or incomplete treatment must come from an established service, relic, or procedure.

Contamination WIL loss follows the recovery rules in **Temporal Contamination**. Ordinary rest does not erase Quirks.

## Deprivation and Fatigue
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-07 | RULE | Deprivation and Fatigue -->
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-08 | WARNING | Full inventory -->

An agent who lacks a crucial need such as food, water, sleep, shelter, or anti-rejection medicine is **Deprived**. A Deprived agent cannot recover HP, attributes, or inventory slots occupied by Fatigue.

After each full day of unresolved deprivation, add one **Fatigue** to the inventory. Rules and events may also add Fatigue immediately. Each Fatigue occupies one slot and cannot be discarded like equipment.

Fatigue remains until the need has been resolved and the agent recuperates, normally with a full night's rest in safety. If Fatigue must be added and no slot is free, the agent must immediately drop, abandon, or hand off an item. An agent cannot carry more than ten occupied slots.

At **ten occupied slots**, current HP becomes 0 and cannot rise above 0 until at least one slot is cleared. This is not damage: it does not cause a Scar or reduce STR. Once a slot is free and the agent is not Deprived, HP can recover normally.

## Armor and Protection
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-09 | RULE | Armor -->

Subtract Armor from a damage roll before applying the remainder to HP. Damage cannot fall below 0. Total Armor is capped at **3**, regardless of how many protective items are worn.

Armor works only when it is worn, functional, and relevant to the attack. Total cover blocks an attack entirely; it is not additional Armor.

| Protection | Armor | Cost | Slots and limits |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Light tactical vest, kevlar II | 1 | Division issue | 0 while the standard issued vest is worn; 1 if packed or carried as an extra |
| Ballistic vest | 2 | $1,500 | Bulky, 2 slots |
| Heavy tactical armor | 3 | $3,000 | Restricted Zhou-network gear; bulky, 2 slots |
| Helmet | +1 | $200 | 1 slot; stacks with body armor to the maximum of 3 |

Protective gear may also change the fiction. A helmet can protect against falling debris; heavy armor may be obvious, slow to conceal, or impossible to wear through a narrow access route.

## Reactions
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-10 | RULE | Reaction rolls -->

When an NPC's immediate attitude is genuinely uncertain, the Warden rolls **2d6**. Established motives, prior treatment, visible weapons, and clear orders may make a roll unnecessary.

| 2d6 | Initial reaction |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Hostile: attacks, raises an alarm, or makes an immediate demand |
| 3-5 | Wary: keeps distance, prepares for trouble, or requires proof |
| 6-8 | Curious: delays judgment and wants information |
| 9-11 | Cooperative: willing to talk, trade, or grant limited help |
| 12 | Helpful: offers meaningful assistance within their interests |

The result establishes an opening position, not permanent obedience. What the agents say, offer, threaten, or reveal changes the conversation from there.

## Morale
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-11 | RULE | Morale -->

Enemies make a **WIL save** to continue fighting when they suffer their first casualty and again when they lose half their original number. A coherent group may use its leader's WIL. A lone enemy saves when reduced to 0 HP.

If two thresholds occur at the same moment, make one morale save, not two. A failed save produces credible self-preservation: retreat, surrender, negotiation, abandonment of the objective, or another response suited to the enemy. It does not require mindless flight.

Morale does not affect player characters. Creatures without self-preservation, disciplined units under exceptional control, and entities whose behavior is already established may ignore morale.

## Combat

Violence is fast because attacks normally hit. The decisive choices usually happen before the damage roll: whether the attack is possible, who is exposed, what cover exists, whether an escape remains open, and what the objective is worth.

## Before the First Round

Before initiative, establish:

- each side's visible position and approximate distance;
- meaningful cover and blocked lines of fire;
- exits and unsafe routes;
- what the opponents appear ready to do;
- whether either side is genuinely unaware, helpless, or already in control.

If one side has achieved an unquestionable ambush or other decisive positional advantage, the Warden may let that side act first without a DEX save. This is not a free extra round. It determines who acts in the first exchange.

## Rounds, Sides, and Initiative
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-12 | RULE | Initiative -->

A round is roughly **10 seconds**. Combat proceeds by sides.

During the **first round**, each PC makes a DEX save unless circumstances have already determined who acts first.

1. PCs who succeed may act.
2. PCs who fail lose their turn for this first round.
3. The opponents act.
4. The round ends.

From the second round onward, all able PCs act, then all opponents act. Within a side, participants declare their intentions before resolving them. The players may resolve their actions in any useful order, but actions within the same side are treated as broadly simultaneous when exact sequencing would create a contradiction.

The Warden telegraphs the opponents' likely actions before the PCs commit whenever those intentions would be apparent. A changing situation may alter side order only when the fiction clearly demands it.

## Movement and Actions
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-13 | RULE | Movement and actions -->

On a turn, a character may move up to approximately **40 feet** and take **one action**. An action may include:

- attacking;
- using an item or relic;
- reloading a firearm;
- administering first aid;
- forcing, climbing, or manipulating something under pressure;
- moving a second time;
- preparing to respond to a clear trigger;
- another reasonable activity of similar scope.

Movement may occur before or after the action, or be split around it when the fiction permits. A prepared action is lost if its stated trigger does not occur before the character's next turn.

There are no automatic attacks of opportunity. Leaving an enemy's reach may still require a save or expose the character to a stated consequence when the enemy can realistically prevent escape.

## Attacking
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-14 | RULE | Automatic attacks -->

An attack succeeds automatically when the attacker can reach the target and the attack is possible. Choose the relevant damage die, roll it, subtract Armor, and apply the remaining damage.

Total cover, an impossible angle, an ineffective weapon, or a target outside meaningful range can block the attack entirely. Do not roll damage for an impossible attack.

### Impaired and Enhanced Attacks
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-15 | RULE | Impaired and enhanced attacks -->

An attack made from a position of weakness is **impaired** and rolls **d4** damage regardless of the weapon. Common causes include firing through substantial cover, attacking while restrained, using a long weapon in a cramped struggle, or shooting at an especially distant target.

An attack made from a decisive position is **enhanced** and rolls **d12** damage regardless of the weapon. Common causes include striking a helpless target, firing from a prepared ambush, or exploiting a dangerous maneuver that has already succeeded.

Impaired and enhanced do not stack. If both apply, they cancel and the attack uses its normal damage die. Several causes of impairment still produce d4; several causes of enhancement still produce d12.

> An automatic hit is not guaranteed bodily injury. HP can absorb the result as movement, luck, damaged cover, or a glancing impact. The attack becomes physically serious when damage reaches an attribute.

### Cover

- **Total cover:** blocks the attack.
- **Substantial cover:** impairs the incoming attack.
- **Concealment without protection:** may make the attack impossible until the target is located, but does not automatically provide Armor.

### Multiple Attackers
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-16 | RULE | Focused fire -->

When multiple attackers on the same side target one foe during the same round, every attacker commits their action. Roll all applicable damage dice and keep the **single highest result**, then subtract the target's Armor once.

This rule prevents focused fire from multiplying damage while preserving the advantage of coordination and larger dice. Separate attacks that occur at different times are resolved separately.

### Dual Weapons

A character attacking with two weapons uses one action, rolls both damage dice, and keeps the single highest result. Carrying two weapons does not create a second attack. If the combined attack is impaired or enhanced, replace both weapon dice with two d4s or two d12s and keep the highest.

### Blast
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-17 | RULE | Blast -->

An attack with **Blast** affects every valid target in its stated area. Roll damage separately for each target and apply Armor separately. Position still matters: a wall may block the blast, and throwing an explosive into a crowded space may endanger allies, civilians, evidence, or the objective.

## Damage Sequence
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-18 | RULE | Damage sequence -->

Resolve each damaging attack in this order.

1. Determine whether the attack is normal, impaired, enhanced, or impossible.
2. Roll the damage die or dice.
3. Subtract relevant Armor, minimum 0.
4. Apply the remaining damage to HP.
5. If HP is reduced to **exactly 0** with no damage remaining, resolve a Scar for a PC.
6. If damage remains after HP reaches 0, subtract the remainder from STR.
7. If STR reaches 0, the target dies. Otherwise, immediately make a STR save using the new current STR.
8. On a failed STR save, the target suffers critical damage.

> **Example:** Chen has 2 HP, 11 current STR, and 1 Armor. A rifle rolls 6 damage. Armor reduces it to 5. Two damage remove Chen's HP; the remaining 3 reduce STR to 8. Chen now makes a STR save against 8. A roll of 9 fails, so he suffers critical damage.

## Critical Damage and Stabilization
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-19 | RULE | Critical damage and stabilization -->

On a successful STR save after taking STR damage, the character remains able to act at 0 HP and the new, lower STR. Any further damage goes directly to STR until HP is recovered, and each new loss requires another STR save.

A character who fails the STR save suffers **critical damage**. They are defeated: they can only crawl, speak weakly, and perform similarly minimal actions. They die within **60 minutes** unless stabilized.

An adjacent character may spend one action and one use from a first-aid kit to stabilize them. No roll is required in relative safety. Under immediate danger, the helper must also make a DEX save; the consequence of failure is stated before the attempt. The first-aid use is spent either way when treatment begins.

Stabilization prevents death from that critical injury. It does not restore HP or STR, remove Deprivation, or make the character ready to fight. A stabilized character requires extraction, rest, and appropriate treatment. Further serious harm to an unstabilized or helpless character may kill them without another save when the fiction makes survival impossible.

Some enemies have an effect triggered when a target **fails** a critical damage save. Apply that effect after the failure unless its description says otherwise.

NPCs and ordinary enemies that suffer critical damage are out of the fight. The Warden decides whether they are dying, unconscious, incapacitated, or capable of being stabilized according to the fiction.

## Attribute Loss at 0

- **STR 0:** death.
- **DEX 0:** paralysis and inability to act until restored through extended treatment or extraordinary means.
- **WIL 0 from contamination:** immediate transformation into a temporal echo; the character leaves player control.
- **WIL 0 from another source:** catatonia or delirium; the character cannot act and requires extraordinary treatment.

## Scars
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-20 | RULE | Scars -->

Only a PC whose HP is reduced to **exactly 0** by one attack and who takes no STR damage receives a Scar. Do not roll a d12 for the Scar. Use the amount of HP lost in that attack, after Armor. If an unusual effect causes 12 or more HP loss while ending at exactly 0, use entry 12.

Unless an entry states otherwise, resolving a Scar does not immediately restore HP. The agent remains at 0 HP until they can recover normally. NPCs do not usually receive Scars, though the Warden may use the table for an important recurring character.

| HP lost | Scar |
|---:|---|
| 1 | **Lasting Scar.** Roll d6 for location: 1 Neck, 2 Hands, 3 Eye, 4 Chest, 5 Legs, 6 Ear. Roll d6; if the result exceeds maximum HP, set maximum HP to the result. |
| 2 | **Rattling Blow.** The agent is disoriented and shaken. Describe how they refocus. Roll d6; if the result exceeds maximum HP, set maximum HP to the result. |
| 3 | **Winded.** The agent is thrown down and becomes Deprived until resting for a few hours. After that rest, roll d6 and add the result to maximum HP. |
| 4 | **Broken Limb.** Roll d6: 1-2 Leg, 3-4 Arm, 5 Rib, 6 Skull. Once mended, roll 2d6; if the result exceeds maximum HP, set maximum HP to the result. |
| 5 | **Infected.** A serious infection develops. When it is overcome, roll 2d6; if the result exceeds maximum HP, set maximum HP to the result. |
| 6 | **Reorienting Head Wound.** Roll d6: 1-2 STR, 3-4 DEX, 5-6 WIL. Roll 3d6. If the result exceeds the attribute's current score, raise the current score to the result; raise its maximum to match if necessary. |
| 7 | **Hamstrung.** The agent can barely move without serious help and rest. After recovery, roll 3d6; if the result exceeds maximum DEX, set maximum DEX and current DEX to the result. |
| 8 | **Deafened.** The agent cannot hear until extraordinary aid restores them. Make a WIL save; on a success, increase maximum WIL by d4. |
| 9 | **Re-brained.** A hidden part of the agent's psyche is knocked loose. Roll 3d6; if the result exceeds maximum WIL, set maximum WIL and current WIL to the result. |
| 10 | **Sundered.** An appendage is torn off, crippled, or rendered useless; the Warden determines which from the attack. Make a WIL save; on a success, increase maximum WIL by d6. |
| 11 | **Mortal Wound.** The agent is Deprived and out of action and dies in one hour unless stabilized. Upon full recovery, roll 2d6 and replace maximum HP with the result. |
| 12 | **Doomed.** The agent narrowly survives. The next failed critical damage save kills them immediately. If that save succeeds, remove Doomed, roll 3d6, and set maximum HP to the result if it is higher. |

A Scar is both a rule and a permanent event. Record what happened, what changed physically or psychologically, and who witnessed it.

## Retreat
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-21 | RULE | Retreat -->

Escaping a dire situation requires a safe destination and a successful **DEX save**. Before the roll, establish what failure means: being cut off, dropping equipment, becoming separated, taking direct attribute damage, or reaching the exit after the enemy.

A character does not need a retreat save when the opposition cannot meaningfully prevent departure. Conversely, a successful roll cannot create a safe route where none exists. The agents must first make escape possible.

## Detachments

A large group of similar combatants can be treated as one **detachment** with one set of attributes and one action per round.

- Individual attacks against a detachment are impaired unless they have Blast or another established area effect.
- Detachment attacks against individuals are enhanced and have Blast.
- Detachment attacks against detachments use their normal damage.
- Critical damage routes, scatters, or significantly weakens a detachment.
- A detachment at STR 0 is destroyed as an effective force.

Use detachments to resolve scale, not to make every crowd a single creature. A few named opponents with different goals should remain individuals.

### Ammo Dice
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-22 | RULE | Ammo Dice -->

Each firearm begins with an **Ammo die**, normally d6. Roll it after:

- sustained or suppressive fire;
- a firefight in which that weapon fired more than once;
- a Blast attack from an automatic weapon;
- another clearly prolonged expenditure.

Do not roll Ammo after a single deliberate shot outside immediate pressure unless the weapon or situation specifically requires it.

On **1-2**, step the Ammo die from d6 to d4. On **1-2** when rolling d4, the weapon becomes empty. On any other result, the die remains unchanged. The attack or firefight that triggered the roll resolves before the die steps down or the weapon becomes empty.

Reloading during immediate danger uses one action. A compatible spare magazine or ammunition bundle is consumed and resets the weapon to d6. Standard resupply costs **$50**. Outside immediate danger, reloading does not require round-by-round resolution, but ammunition must still be available.

## Death and Replacement
<!-- UTRULE: CORE-23 | RULE | Death and replacement -->

A dead or transformed agent leaves player control. The player creates a replacement or takes control of an available allied character, joining at the first plausible opportunity rather than waiting for the next session.

A new replacement begins as a **Recruit** with standard equipment, $300 Cash, $1,200 Debt, Loyalty 5, Corruption 0, Exposure 0, Certificates 0, and no Quirks.

Personal Cash and personal Debt do not transfer unless the fiction establishes an heir, guarantor, creditor, or other claim. Obligations explicitly recorded on the team campaign sheet remain. The lost agent's body, equipment, evidence, secrets, and relationships continue to exist in the world and may shape the replacement's arrival.
