> A **four-session, ten-week** mini-campaign (2-3 table hours per session) showing how all systems interact: economy, contamination, loyalty, corruption, and moral choices. Unlike the Chicago Loop (single adventure), this campaign makes you **feel time passing.** Debt grows. Zhou knocks. Contamination accumulates. Characters change, literally.

The session divisions are pacing suggestions. Advance the calendar only through actual missions and downtime. A session may span several accounting weeks, but it never automatically grants recovery, resets exposure or creates a Quirk.

## Preparation

PCs are freshly arrived recruits. Create them normally. The Warden keeps an **accounting sheet** for each PC: Cash, Debt, Certificates, pay, expenses, loyalty, corruption, contamination, Quirks. Update between sessions. Players must see the numbers. The spiral is more effective when it's transparent.

## Session 1: The Arrival (Weeks 1-2)

### Scene 1: Welcome Briefing

Hayes receives the PCs in her office. Efficient, cold, professional. Key information:

- Pay: $800/week, paid Monday
- Expenses: rent $500, food $150, anti-rejection medicine $250. Total: $900
- The deficit is $100/week. Hayes doesn't mention it. PCs discover it when they do the math
- Standard equipment distributed
- First mission tomorrow

> Hayes isn't evil. She's a bureaucrat doing her job. When a PC asks "how do you survive on $800 a week?", Hayes answers: "With discipline and priorities, agent." She's not lying. It simply isn't her problem.

### Scene 2: First Mission (Yellow Zone)

**Objective:** Recover a crate of biological samples from a warehouse in the Yellow Zone, 12 km from base.

**The zone:** Semi-abandoned residential neighborhood. Buildings "jump" between eras. The air is thick. Time flows irregularly.

**Contamination:** 1 WIL save after 8 hours in zone (Yellow Zone standard). With the suit (reduces WIL damage by 1), risk is low but real.

**Complication (Warden's choice):**

- **Option A (social):** Inside the warehouse is a civilian family taking shelter. They have medicine. The crate is under their belongings. Taking it leaves them without shelter.
- **Option B (tactical):** The warehouse is watched by 2 armed scavengers (Yellow Zone bandits, 4 HP, 10 STR, 12 DEX, 8 WIL, pistol d6). Fight is risky. Negotiate is possible. Flanking costs time.
- **Option C (moral):** The crate also contains a small unregistered Oculus fragment. Nobody knows it's there. Quoted at $3,000 black-market purchasing value (**3 Certificates** through Zhou); ordinary Cash requires a separate cash-out transaction. PCs discover it during transport.

**End of mission:** A field mission pays the $50 allowance. A normal completion is a **standard success**: $200 gross, $140 after the 30% deduction, for **$190 total mission pay**. +1 Loyalty if they hand everything over.

**Worked Week 1 close:** Start $500 Cash / $500 Debt. Add $800 pay + $190 mission pay. Pay $900 fixed costs and a $50 example unexpected expense. Cash closes at $540. With no repayment, Debt becomes **$525** after interest.

**Worked Week 2 close:** No mission. Add $800 pay, then pay $900 fixed + $100 unexpected. Cash closes at $340. No repayment: Debt becomes **$552**.

## Session 2: The Pressure (Weeks 3-4)

### Scene 1: Two Missions Offered

Hayes offers two missions. PCs can choose one (can't do both, same time window).

**Mission A: Yellow Zone, routine.** Escort a Division tech installing temporal seismic sensors. A clean completion is a **standard success**: $190 total mission pay.

**Mission B: Red Zone, dangerous.** Data recovery from a collapsed lab on the Strip. 3 confirmed temporal echoes. A full recovery is an **exceptional success**: $400 total mission pay. Contamination is severe (a check every 3 hours, d4). The planned 6-hour route reaches two periodic checks from a fresh clock; a faster successful exit can avoid the second. Telegraph delays and record actual elapsed exposure.

Mission B pays more, but not enough to make the temporal risk trivial.

### Scene 2: Zhou's Offer

When a PC's **Debt reaches $700+**, Zhou makes contact. In the worked no-repayment example, Week 3 closes at $580 Debt and Week 4 at **$735**. Repayments, clemency, or extra income can delay this; that variance is intentional.

> "Dear Agent [name], I've noticed the Division doesn't sufficiently appreciate your services. I have a small assignment that could solve your financial problems. Nothing dangerous. Nothing illegal (technically). $1,500 for one hour of your time. You already know my address. Everyone in Vegas does.
>
> With regards, M. Zhou"

If the PC goes: the job is simple. Carry a sealed package from point A to point B. Don't open it, don't ask questions. Pay: $1,500 cash. Corruption: +1.

If the PC refuses, Zhou doesn't insist. She waits. The offers get worse as debt grows.

### Scene 3: Side Gig

Benson casually mentions a Yellow Zone bar looking for a weekend bouncer. $200 cash. Weekend shifts occupy two days. They interrupt any continuous medical treatment spanning those days, but do not prevent brief HP recovery with rest and water outside the shifts. No automatic Corruption.

**Worked Weeks 3-4:** Week 3 standard success with $150 unexpected closes at $280 Cash / $580 Debt. Week 4 with no mission and a $300 emergency exhausts Cash, adds $120 unpaid costs, then interest produces **$735 Debt**. Zhou's $1,500 can erase it immediately; refusing carries the pressure forward.

## Session 3: The Fall (Weeks 5-7)

This is the session where the game bares its teeth.

### Scene 1: The Mission Goes Wrong

Hayes issues a direct order to investigate a Red Zone after a previous squad vanished. Before the PCs decide, she states the consequences of refusing this assignment: **-2 Loyalty once**, no allowance for an unperformed mission, and a contract review. Also record one Mission Absence; do not invent an immediate dismissal beyond that procedure. The PCs can accept, demand support, propose reconnaissance first, obtain medical exemption when genuinely unfit, or refuse.

**The zone:** Underground laboratory beneath the Luxor. Phase 2 instability. Severe contamination (WIL save every 3 hours, d4 damage). 4-6 temporal echoes. A corridor where time flows backward.

**What they find:** Bodies of 2 agents from the previous squad. 1 surviving agent, hidden, traumatized, with 2 visible Quirks. The data, intact, in an armored briefcase. A class-B Oculus fragment (Hayes didn't mention this).

**The opposition:** 4 temporal echoes (4 HP, 8 STR, 12 DEX, 6 WIL, temporal claw d6, WIL save on critical damage or accelerated aging). They block the direct route to the briefcase, but do not teleport into a mandatory fight. Show their patrol pattern, the backward-flowing corridor and a retreat route. Resolve any encounter without altering their strength to force a loss.

> **To the Warden:** This is a deliberately lethal situation, not a scheduled death. Telegraph the odds, preserve credible escape routes, and apply the consequences of the players' actions. At 0 STR a PC dies; avoiding the fight or returning without the briefcase can be a valid outcome.

### Scene 2: Replacement

If a PC dies, the player creates a new character (15 minutes). The new PC starts at Recruit tier with $500 personal Debt and normal starting Cash ($500, or $1,000 for a Con Artist). They do not inherit the dead PC's personal Debt. Explicit team obligations remain with the team. Hayes is pragmatic: "We lost Agent [name]. Your new colleague is Agent [new name]. Briefing at 06:00."

### Scene 3: The Fragment Dilemma

Hayes asks for the data. The PCs decide what to deliver and how to explain any missing material. The Oculus fragment is not in any report. If PCs mention it, Hayes takes it without comment. If they don't mention it, they have an unregistered class-B fragment. Zhou would pay $5,000. The Division doesn't know it exists.

Successful delivery grants +1 Loyalty once for the mission, not again for each item. Keeping the fragment adds no automatic Zhou-network Corruption; discovery can cost -2 Loyalty and confiscation after a hearing. A sale to Zhou follows the relic-sale procedure and agreed payment. The $5,000 quote here is black-market purchasing value, normally **5 Certificates**, not an automatic Cash payment.

## Session 4: Who Are You? (Weeks 8-10)

The final session is less action, more consequences.

### Scene 1: Zhou Collects

If anyone worked for Zhou before, she asks a second, bigger favor. The PC who said yes the first time is in a weak position: Zhou knows things. If nobody worked for Zhou, she raises the stakes to $3,000-$5,000. For a PC with $1,500 in debt and eviction imminent, that changes everything.

**Zhou's job (if they accept):** Sabotage a Division convoy. Don't kill anyone, just delay it. Zhou wants to reach an Oculus fragment first. Corruption: +3. If discovered: fired from the Division (game over for that PC, unless they work for Zhou full-time).

### Scene 2: The Division Demands

Hayes convenes the group. Monthly results are "below expectations." Translation: the Division spent more on them than they recovered. Hayes offers a final bonus mission: Red Zone, high risk, high reward (exceptional $500 gross band: $350 after deduction, plus the $50 field allowance). The $400 payout helps the ledger, but does not guarantee that every agent breaks even. If they refuse, the Division "reevaluates their contract."

A veiled threat. Not of death: of termination. A fired Division agent loses salary and institutional access, but can still seek independent work, buy treatment or bargain for another arrangement; make those costs concrete.

### Scene 3: The Final Choice

Possible combinations:

- **Division mission + refuse Zhou:** Division income and its risks; actual poverty and contamination depend on the ledger and exposure
- **Division mission + work for Zhou:** Playing both sides. Enormous risk, enormous potential gain
- **Zhou only:** Betray the Division. High corruption, money, but no safety net
- **Neither:** Decline both, negotiate terms or seek independent work. Apply the existing absence record and faction thresholds; termination and loss of medicine are not automatic scene outcomes

There is no right ending. There is the ending the PCs choose and the consequences that follow.

## Final Accounting (Example)

A single Recruit starts at Loyalty 5, refuses Zhou, claims no clemency and makes **no voluntary Debt repayments**. Each completed Division mission grants +1 Loyalty, capped at 10. There are no other Loyalty changes. Week 5's Strong band improves to Exceptional at Loyalty 7; Week 7's Standard improves to Strong at Loyalty 8. The salary increase earned at Loyalty 9 applies from Week 8. The PC has only six successful missions by Week 10, so has not yet qualified for Agent rank.

| Week and paid outcome | Income | Costs | Closing Cash | Closing Debt |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Start | - | - | $500 | $500 |
| 1: Standard | $990 | $950 | $540 | $525 |
| 2: no mission | $800 | $1,000 | $340 | $552 |
| 3: Standard | $990 | $1,050 | $280 | $580 |
| 4: no mission | $800 | $1,200 | $0 | $735 |
| 5: Strong -> Exceptional | $1,200 | $1,000 | $200 | $772 |
| 6: no mission | $800 | $950 | $50 | $811 |
| 7: Standard -> Strong | $1,060 | $1,100 | $10 | $852 |
| 8: Exceptional; Hero base pay | $1,600 | $950 | $660 | $895 |
| 9: no mission; Hero base pay | $1,200 | $1,000 | $860 | $940 |
| 10: Standard -> Strong; Hero base pay | $1,460 | $1,050 | $1,270 | $987 |

**What this example shows:** the $700 Debt threshold is reached in Week 4, but high Loyalty can later release financial pressure before promotion. Debt still grows because the player has chosen not to repay it, despite accumulating Cash. Repayment, clemency, side work, failed missions and faction conflict change this path. Honest success is not sabotaged to preserve a predetermined spiral.

The costs include the listed weekly expenses and example contingencies only. No unrecorded treatment, equipment or civilian-rescue surcharge is assumed. The table is a worked ledger, not a claim that the ten fictional weeks will unfold this way.

> *What did you accept, what did you refuse, and who can now ask you for more?*
